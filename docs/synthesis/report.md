# Phase 25 — Synthetic ECG augmentation for rare classes

Generated by `scripts/rare_class_power.py` and `scripts/synth_ablation.py`. Raw results:
[`power.json`](power.json), [`ablation.json`](ablation.json).

**Result: synthetic augmentation did not help. Neither did any other kind.** All four
augmentation arms scored *below* the un-augmented baseline on the rare classes, and the
generator — which is not memorizing and not mode-collapsed — produces recordings whose
morphology is wrong in exactly the way that matters. The mechanism is measurable, and it is
the useful part of this phase.

---

## 1. The question could not be asked as posed

The brief asks to augment PTB-XL's rarest classes and measure AUROC on them. Those labels
cannot support that measurement. The 17 labels with fewer than 50 training examples have
**one to five positives in the official test fold** — `PRC(S)` has 1, `2AVB` has 1, `3AVB`
has 2.

The usual check hides this. A percentile bootstrap over test records reports those labels as
measured *more tightly* than the ones used below:

| Cohort | median test positives | bootstrap 95% CI width | Hanley-McNeil 95% CI width |
|---|---|---|---|
| 17 labels with <50 train examples | 2 | **0.035** | **0.373** |
| 8 induced-rarity targets | 51 | 0.041 | 0.093 |

The bootstrap is wrong, and instructively so: with two positives among 2,198 records, every
resample reuses the same two patients and reshuffles 2,196 negatives, so the interval
measures variability in the negatives. It cannot resample the thing that is scarce.
Hanley-McNeil, whose standard error depends explicitly on the positive count, puts the rare
labels **4.0x wider**. A ±0.19 interval swallows any augmentation effect whole.

So rarity is **induced** on 8 labels that do have test support: 50 real annotations kept per
target, the rest masked. The label becomes rare in the way that matters — the model sees 50
examples — while the test fold keeps its 40–112 positives.

## 2. Setup

3 seeds · 50 real examples kept per target · 8 targets · generator 20 epochs · classifier
10 epochs · every arm adds **350 rows per target**, so the arms differ in *how* the rows
were made and in nothing else.

| Arm | What it adds |
|---|---|
| `baseline` | nothing |
| `oversample` | the 50 real examples, repeated |
| `classical` | the 50 real examples, repeated and perturbed |
| `synthetic` | diffusion samples conditioned on the rare label |
| `synthetic+classical` | diffusion samples, perturbed |

The generator is retrained per seed **on the masked labels**. Training it on the full label
matrix would smuggle the withheld annotations back in through the samples, and the
"improvement" would measure the leak. A regression test asserts no arm can restore a masked
annotation.

Paired bootstrap, per target, CI excluding zero:

- `oversample` — 3 of 8 (two are +0.003 on labels already at 0.99; one is **−0.045** on LAO/LAE)
- `classical` — 1 of 8 (+0.004 on CLBBB)
- `synthetic` — **3 of 8, all harmful**: ILMI −0.026, ISCAL −0.019, QWAVE −0.027
- `synthetic+classical` — 1 of 8 (ISCAL −0.021)

Most individual differences are not significant, which is itself worth stating: at this
sample size the honest summary is "no arm demonstrably helped, and the synthetic arm is the
only one with several significant *harms*."

## 4. Why it failed — the generator is ECG-shaped but not an ECG

The generator passes every check that generative-model papers usually report:

| Check | Value | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Memorization (NN-distance ratio vs held-out real) | **1.083** | not copying its training data |
| Diversity ratio | **1.108** | no mode collapse |
| Delineable QRS | **100%** | every sample looks like a rhythm strip |

And fails the one that matters. Measured with Phase 22's delineator, pooled over all seeds
and targets:

| Measure | Synthetic | Real | Error |
|---|---|---|---|
| QRS duration | 227 ms | 112 ms | **+115 ms** |
| PR interval | 222 ms | 183 ms | +38 ms |
| QT interval | 383 ms | 360 ms | +22 ms |
| Heart rate | 91 bpm | 77 bpm | +14 bpm |
| P wave detected | **31%** | **85%** | −54 pts |

A QRS complex twice as wide as it should be, and a P wave missing two thirds of the time.
The diffusion model has learned the *low-frequency envelope* of an ECG — the right marginal
variance, a plausible beat cadence, believable amplitudes — and not the sharp deflections
that carry the diagnosis. Its samples are smeared.

That explains the direction of the result rather than merely accompanying it. The targets
are defined by exactly the morphology the generator gets wrong: CLBBB and CRBBB **are** a
wide QRS, `QWAVE` is a specific narrow deflection, `PAC` is a premature *P* wave in a model
that produces a detectable P only 31% of the time. Training on these samples does not add
weak examples of the class; it adds examples that contradict the class definition. The two
labels where synthetic did least damage (CLBBB, CRBBB, both ≈0.99 already) are the ones a
model can still get right from features other than the exact QRS width.

**Oversampling was the worst arm overall** (−0.0228), which is its own small lesson: adding
350 duplicates of 50 recordings, per label, across 8 labels, inflates the training set by
16% with content carrying no new information, and the model overfits it. The cheapest
intervention is not free.

## 5. What would have to change

- **The generator needs a high-frequency objective.** An MSE denoising loss on a signal
  dominated by low-frequency content will always under-weight the 10–40 Hz band the QRS
  lives in. A spectrally-weighted loss, or generation in a wavelet/STFT domain, targets the
  failure directly.
- **Condition on measurements, not just labels.** The delineator already extracts PR/QRS/QT
  (Phase 22). Conditioning on them, and rejecting samples whose measured intervals fall
  outside the class's physiologic range, is a filter available today — sample quality could
  be enforced rather than hoped for.
- **Check morphology before running the ablation.** The 8-epoch probe already showed P waves
  in 4.7% of samples. That number, not downstream AUROC, was the signal that this generator
  was not ready, and it costs minutes rather than hours to obtain.

## 6. Limitations

- **One generator family, one budget.** A conditional DDPM at 20 epochs on 17k records. A
  larger model, longer training, or a published ECG-specific architecture (ECGAN, SSSD-ECG)
  may well do better; this is evidence about *this* generator, not about synthetic ECG
  augmentation in general.
- **One rarity level.** 50 kept examples. The effect may differ at 10 or 200, and a
  scarcity curve would be more informative than a single point.
- **Induced rarity is not real rarity.** Masking makes a label scarce but leaves its
  recordings in the training set with their other labels intact, and real rare conditions
  may be rare for reasons that also make them harder.
- **Underpowered per target.** 40–112 test positives gives wide intervals; only the macro
  average across 8 targets and 3 seeds is reasonably stable.
- **The genuinely rare labels remain unmeasured**, as §1 explains, and no result here should
  be read as applying to them.
