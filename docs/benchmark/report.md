The hosted arms are implemented and one command away:

```bash
OPENAI_API_KEY=... make benchmark-foundation
```

They are recorded as *not run* rather than omitted. An empty column with an explanation is
honest; an absent column reads as an oversight, and a filled-in column that quietly came
from somewhere else is worse than either.

**The local Qwen arm is not a stand-in for GPT-4o.** It is a 1.5B general-purpose model; GPT-4o
is orders of magnitude larger and multimodal. What the Qwen arm establishes is the shape of
the specialist/generalist gap on this task and the cost of the protocol, not a number that
transfers to a frontier model.

## How a language model is shown an ECG

A text model cannot read a `(12, 1000)` array, so the protocol has to be chosen and stated.
This benchmark uses the **measurement-mediated** protocol: the recording is reduced to the
numbers a cardiologist reads off it — rate, PR, QRS, QT/QTc, per-lead ST levels, all from
Phase 22's delineator — and handed over as text. The model returns a 0–10 score for each of
PTB-XL's five diagnostic superclasses plus one sentence of interpretation.

This is **deliberately generous to the generalist**. It is not asked to find a QRS complex
in a picture; it is given the interval already measured by APEX's own front end. Any gap
that survives that handicap cannot be explained by feature extraction.

It also **under-states** what a strong multimodal model could do, because measurements
discard morphology — the shape of a Q wave, an rSR′ pattern, T-wave contour. The cited
image-based GPT-4o figures are the relevant anchor for that setting, and they are lower
still (~41% multiclass accuracy).

### The generalist arm is indistinguishable from chance

0.4681 looks like "worse than random", and that would be over-reading it. Every
superclass confidence interval contains 0.5:

| | AUROC | n pos | 95% CI (Hanley-McNeil) |
|---|---|---|---|
| NORM | 0.473 | 80 | [0.380, 0.566] |
| MI | 0.514 | 28 | [0.394, 0.634] |
| STTC | 0.410 | 34 | [0.305, 0.514] |
| CD | 0.446 | 32 | [0.336, 0.556] |
| HYP | 0.498 | 12 | [0.327, 0.669] |

The honest statement is **no measurable discrimination**, not anti-correlation. The model
answered every cell it was asked for (100% coverage) — it is fluent and compliant, and its
numbers carry no signal.

### Self-contradiction, and why its denominator matters

APEX cannot contradict itself: the report is rendered from the detections by a template, so
its text and its scores are the same object. That 0.0% is over **n=2,189 records whose text
asserted a category** — a real denominator.

The language model's is not. **Only 14 of its 150 replies named a category at all** — the
other 91% are hedged prose that asserts nothing and cannot contradict anything. Of those 14,
all 14 contradicted the model's own scores.

So "100% self-contradiction" is true and needs its denominator to be read correctly: it
describes 14 records, not 150. The first version of this metric reported the rate alone, and
a 12-record probe showed a "100%" resting on a *single* record. A bare rate in a comparison
table reads as a damning, well-supported number; this one is a real defect on a small
sample. The metric now carries its `n`, and a test enforces it.

The hedging is itself a finding. A model that declines to commit 91% of the time is not
safe-by-default — it answered all 750 score cells with confident numbers while its prose
refused to say anything, which is the worst combination: a number a downstream system will
act on, and text that gives a human reader nothing to check it against.

The failure mode is real even where it is rare. From the probe:

> **scores:** NORM 0.9, MI 0.0, STTC 0.8, CD 0.0, HYP 0.0
> **text:** "The ECG shows a predominantly normal rhythm with no evidence of ischemic changes…"

and, from the first smoke run, a reply that answered **`MI: 9/10`** immediately above the
sentence *"There is no evidence of myocardial ischemia."* No accuracy metric sees that. It
is the kind of defect that reaches a clinician looking authoritative.

## Where the specialist wins, and where it would not

**Wins, measured here:** discrimination (0.89 vs no measurable signal), latency (~900×),
cost (~15,000× against projected GPT-4o), privacy (nothing leaves the host — the decisive
column for PHI, where an API arm needs a BAA, a data-flow review, and a vendor retention
policy), and self-consistency by construction.

**Where the comparison is not settled:**

- **A frontier multimodal model was not run.** Everything above about "the generalist"
  is measured on a 1.5B local model. GPT-4o may do materially better; the cited image
  study (~41% multiclass) suggests it would still trail a specialist, but that is a
  citation, not this benchmark's result.
- **Rare classes cannot be compared at all.** APEX's median AUROC on the 17 labels with
  fewer than 50 training examples is 0.971 — and the median Hanley-McNeil CI width on those
  labels is **0.373** (median 2 test positives). Phase 25 established that this dataset
  cannot measure rare-class differences; that limit applies to this comparison too, and a
  rare-class column would be decoration.
- **The distilled student beats its teacher** (0.8944 vs 0.8929) at 1/35th the size and
  2.5× the speed, reproducing Phase 19's finding that the shipped model is
  over-parameterized. On this evidence the student is the better default, and the only
  reason it is not is that no phase has yet re-validated the whole pipeline on it.
- **Generalists are not competing on this axis in practice.** A foundation model's value in
  a clinical product is drafting, summarizing, translating, and answering follow-up
  questions — the things Phases 6, 23, and 27 use one for. Reading the waveform is the
  specialist's job. This table argues for the split, not for either model in isolation.

## Limitations

- One protocol (measurements), one prompt, one decoding setting; no prompt engineering,
  few-shot examples, or chain-of-thought was attempted for the generalist, any of which
  could move its numbers.
- The generalist arm is 150 records against the specialists' 2,198 — the arms are not
  equally powered, and the CIs above are the honest expression of that.
- Latency is single-record on one machine (Apple Silicon, CPU for APEX, MPS for the LLM),
  with no batching, quantization, or server tuning on either side.
- Prices are list rates on a date, not negotiated rates, and exclude the engineering cost
  of running either option.
