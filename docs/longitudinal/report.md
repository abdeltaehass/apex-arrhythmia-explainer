# Phase 22 — Longitudinal ECG comparison

Generated by `scripts/longitudinal_eval.py` (numbers) and
`scripts/longitudinal_examples.py` (worked cases). Raw results: [`eval.json`](eval.json),
[`examples.md`](examples.md).

Single-ECG analysis asks *what is wrong with this tracing*. Serial comparison asks *what is
different about it*, and in practice the second question drives more decisions than the
first — new ST depression since yesterday means something that ST depression alone does
not. This phase builds that comparison, and measures how much harder it is.

The short version: **on identical data with the identical detector, detecting what changed
scores F1 0.32 against F1 0.64 for detecting what is present.** Differencing two noisy
observations costs exactly half the performance. Most of this report is about where that
cost comes from and how much of it can be bought back.

---

## 1. The cohort

PTB-XL is normally treated as 21,799 independent recordings. 2,111 of its patients were
recorded more than once:

| | |
|---|---|
| Patients with ≥2 recordings | 2,111 |
| Records involved | 5,041 |
| Consecutive prior→current pairs | **2,930** |
| Median gap | 10 days |
| Held-out (fold 10) pairs | 294 |

| Gap | Pairs |
|---|---|
| <24 h | 327 |
| 1–7 d | 992 |
| 8–30 d | 545 |
| 31–365 d | 766 |
| >1 y | 300 |

Pairs are formed **consecutively** (1→2, 2→3), not all-against-all: a clinician compares a
follow-up to the most recent prior, and all-pairs would count the same patient's drift
repeatedly. PTB-XL assigns `strat_fold` per *patient*, so no pair can straddle the
train/test split — asserted in code rather than assumed, because a pair that leaked would
evaluate the detector against its own training data.

**73.1% of pairs have a different SCP code set.** That number does not behave the way a
measure of clinical change should, which is the subject of section 3.

## 2. Measuring intervals, and whether the measurements mean anything

PTB-XL ships no interval annotations, so PR/QRS/QT/QTc and per-lead ST levels are measured
from the waveform: median beat across quality-filtered beats → cubic-spline interpolation
to an effective 500 Hz → global delineation on the spatial-magnitude signal across the
eight independent leads. Baseline is removed by two-stage median filtering (200 ms then
600 ms) rather than the detector's 0.5 Hz high-pass, which visibly distorts the ST segment
this module has to read.

Of 5,041 records, **9 were unmeasurable** and 92 flagged noisy. A P wave was found in 84.7%
overall — and, importantly, in only ~40% of atrial fibrillation records against ~95% of
sinus rhythm, which is the behaviour you want from something that must refuse to report a
PR interval when there is no P wave to measure.

### Validated against labels the delineator never sees

Diagnoses whose definition pins an interval give free ground truth. Held-out fold 10,
longitudinal subset (588 records), AUROC of the **raw measurement alone**:

| Check | AUROC | Median (pos) | Median (neg) | n pos |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PR → first-degree AV block | 0.838 | 229 ms | 175 ms | 32 |
| QRS → complete RBBB | 0.934 | 167 ms | 89 ms | 17 |
| QRS → complete LBBB | 0.921 | 153 ms | 88 ms | 24 |
| HR → sinus bradycardia | 0.947 | 51 bpm | 78 bpm | 19 |
| HR → sinus tachycardia | 0.918 | 115 bpm | 76 bpm | 23 |
| QRS → IVCD | 0.662 | 100 ms | 89 ms | 26 |

A single scalar recovers bundle branch block at AUROC 0.93 and first-degree AV block at
0.84. IVCD is weak, which is expected — PTB-XL's IVCD is a heterogeneous catch-all that
includes incomplete blocks. Long-QT was too rare in this subset (n=4) to score.

**One tuning decision, made on training folds.** With a symmetric 8%-of-peak velocity
threshold for both QRS onset and offset, median QRS in RBBB came out at 121 ms against a
diagnosis that *requires* ≥120 ms — only 52% of RBBB records cleared their own definitional
bar (AUROC 0.782). RBBB's terminal slurred S wave is slow and low-amplitude, exactly what a
velocity threshold truncates. Halving the offset threshold to 4% fixed it without breaking
anything else; pushing to 2% kept helping RBBB while driving median LBBB "QRS" to an
impossible 256 ms, i.e. the search had run into the T wave. 4% is the last value where
every diagnostic group stays physiologically plausible.

The sweep ran on a 3,000-record sample of folds 1–8 (RBBB AUROC 0.782 at 8%, 0.885 at 4%)
and was confirmed on **all 2,198 records of fold 10**, where it gives RBBB AUROC **0.913**,
normal QRS 85 ms, and only 0.5% of normal records falsely ≥120 ms. The table above is a
different, smaller cohort — the 588 fold-10 records that appear in a longitudinal pair —
which is why its RBBB figure (0.934) differs; both are held out, neither was tuned on.

### What the numbers are not

These are 100 Hz recordings low-passed at 40 Hz. Absolute durations carry a bias — QRS
onset and offset are softened by the filter. That bias is acceptable *here specifically*
because a comparison reports a difference measured by the identical algorithm on both
studies, and a constant bias cancels in the subtraction. What does not cancel is the random
error, which is section 4's problem.

## 3. The same-day trap — why the obvious null cohort is not one

The natural way to calibrate a noise floor is PTB-XL's 327 pairs recorded under 24 h apart.
Hours are too short for real remodelling, so their spread should be pure noise.

It is not. **The unconditioned spread is larger at <24 h than at >1 year:**

| Gap | Pairs | QRS SD (all pairs) | QRS RC (label-stable only) | Any acute/arrhythmia code | Both studies normal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| <24 h | 327 | **40.8 ms** | 7.3 ms | **63.0%** | 16.5% |
| 1–30 d | 1,537 | 33.5 ms | 9.6 ms | 66.2% | 14.2% |
| 31–365 d | 766 | 36.7 ms | 7.3 ms | 53.4% | 24.3% |
| >1 y | 300 | **27.1 ms** | 7.0 ms | **34.0%** | 46.3% |

Noise cannot shrink as time passes, so the <24 h cohort is not measuring noise. The right
column says why: **you only get two ECGs within a day if something is acutely wrong.**
Acute or arrhythmic codes appear in 63% of same-day pairs against 34% of year-apart pairs,
and the fraction where both studies are normal nearly triples in the other direction. The
same-day cohort is the *sickest* cohort in the dataset, not a null.

Conditioned on label stability (third column) the floor is flat across every bucket —
7.3 / 9.6 / 7.3 / 7.0 ms — which is what a noise estimate should look like.

This also reframes the 73% label-change rate from section 1: it is **72.2% even at zero
elapsed time**, and *lower* at >1 year (61.7%) than same-day. Some of that is genuine fast
change (16404→16408 is atrial fibrillation reverting to sinus rhythm in 53 minutes), but
much of it is annotation variability — "sinus arrhythmia" vs "sinus rhythm" one minute
apart, or the same ischaemia relabelled to a neighbouring territory. Any evaluation that
treats an SCP code-set difference as ground truth for clinical change inherits that noise,
which is why section 6's numbers are a floor rather than an estimate.

## 4. The noise floor, measured two ways

Since the same-day cohort is unusable, the floor is bracketed:

**Lower bound — within-record split-half.** One recording measured twice from alternating
beats. No disease change is possible; this is instrument noise alone.

**Operational estimate — between-session, label-stable pairs.** Two sessions with an
identical code set. Includes electrode placement, posture, autonomic state. Thresholds are
fitted here, on **folds 1–8 only**.

| Measure | Split-half RC | Fitted RC (folds 1–8) | **Threshold used** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heart rate | 0.6 bpm | 15.7 bpm | 20 bpm |
| PR | 28.0 ms | 29.1 ms | 30 ms |
| QRS | 4.9 ms | 8.7 ms | 10 ms |
| QT | 8.7 ms | 35.0 ms | 40 ms |
| QTc (Bazett) | 8.1 ms | 34.1 ms | 35 ms |
| QTc (Fridericia) | 8.4 ms | 27.3 ms | 30 ms |
| ST level, per lead | 0.032 mV | 0.049 mV | **0.070 mV** |

Three things worth reading off this table.

**Robust statistics are not optional.** Spread is summarised as `1.96 × 1.4826 × MAD`, not
`1.96 × SD`. A few percent of records suffer gross delineation failure and those outliers
dominate: split-half QRS has an SD-based RC of 61.3 ms against a robust 4.9 ms. An SD-based
bar would mean nothing short of bundle branch block could ever be reported.

**Bazett manufactures change.** Between sessions, QTc(Bazett) repeats to 34.1 ms against
Fridericia's 27.3 ms — but *within* a single recording, where the heart rate cannot vary,
the two are identical (8.1 vs 8.4 ms). The entire excess is rate-induced: Bazett divides by
√RR, so a rate change alone moves QTc even when QT is untouched. Bazett — and the raw QT
with it — is therefore suppressed whenever the rate itself moved, and Fridericia carries
the verdict.

**The ST channel needs a multiplicity correction.** Eight leads are tested at once. At a
per-lead 95% bar the chance of at least one false positive on an unchanged tracing is
1 − 0.95⁸ = **34%**, so a third of all comparisons would invent a regional ST change. The
per-lead threshold is widened to hold family-wise error at 5% (z from 1.96 to 2.73),
which is why 0.049 mV of measured spread becomes a 0.070 mV threshold. Gold example
4097→4103 carried a spurious "new ST elevation in V3" before this correction and was clean
after — against a cardiologist who wrote "not significantly changed".

## 5. What the module refuses to say

A change report that goes quiet about what it could not assess reads as "unchanged", so
non-comparisons are stated explicitly. Three suppression rules, each from a real failure:

- **No P wave → no PR.** In atrial fibrillation there is no PR interval. "PR unchanged"
  would be actively misleading.
- **AV dissociation → no PR.** Harder, because a P wave *is* detected and a plausible
  number does come out. Gold pair 7658→7688 developed complete heart block and the module
  reported "PR decreased from 278 ms to 180 ms" for a patient whose atria and ventricles
  had stopped communicating. The guard fires on a surfaced 2AVB/3AVB label — and in that
  very case the detector did not surface one, so it did not help. **The guard is only as
  good as the rhythm call behind it.**
- **Rate change → no raw QT, no Bazett.** QT shortens as rate rises; that is physiology.
  Gold pair 8450→8475 went 72 → 128 bpm and the raw QT duly "decreased by 69 ms" while the
  corrected value barely moved.

A measured change is also distinguished from an *interpretive* one: an ST segment that
shifts 0.06 mV to −0.07 mV has moved detectably but remains within normal limits, and is
reported as such rather than as "new ST depression". Detectability and abnormality are
different questions with different thresholds (the measured noise floor, and the
conventional 0.1 mV / 1 mm).

Every narrative passes a **longitudinal consistency gate** — the Phase-7 idea extended to
change. It is stricter in one way: it checks the *direction* of each interval claim, not
just its subject, because saying the QT shortened when it lengthened is the opposite
clinical message rather than a wording slip. All 12 worked examples pass.

## 6. How well change is actually detected

### Measurement channel

Held-out fold 10, AUROC of the measured delta against the annotation newly gaining that
label:

| Check | AUROC | n pos |
|---|---|---|
| ΔHR → newly labelled sinus tachycardia | 0.824 | 10 |
| ΔPR → newly labelled 1AVB | — | 3 (underpowered) |
| ΔQRS → newly labelled CLBBB | — | 3 (underpowered) |

Only one check clears the power floor. 294 held-out pairs simply do not contain enough
newly-acquired conduction disease to score it — a real limit of this dataset for this task,
and the honest reading is that the measurement channel is validated on *absolute* accuracy
(section 2, where n is adequate) and demonstrated only anecdotally on change.

### Diagnostic channel — the headline

294 held-out pairs, calibrated probabilities (Phase 17's vector scaler, applied here even
though serving still does not use it):

| Task | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New onset**, naive independent thresholding | 0.478 | 0.242 | **0.322** |
| **New onset**, Δp-gated (>0.25) | 0.509 | 0.228 | 0.315 |
| **Resolved**, naive | 0.450 | 0.237 | 0.310 |
| *Static single-ECG detection, same model, same records* | *0.803* | *0.532* | ***0.640*** |

**Change detection scores F1 0.32 where static detection on the identical data scores
0.64.** Differencing two independent threshold crossings roughly doubles the error rate,
and that is the whole story of why this task is harder — not that change is subtler, but
that a difference of two noisy decisions is noisier than either.

The Δp gate was intended to fix exactly that by requiring the underlying probability to
move, not just the decision to flip. **It does not improve F1** (0.322 → 0.315). It trades
recall for precision — 16 fewer false positives, 5 fewer true positives — leaving overall
discrimination unchanged. That is a negative result for the mechanism as a discrimination
improvement, but it is still the operating point worth shipping: a fabricated "new onset of
anterior ischaemia" costs a clinician far more than a missed borderline flicker, and the
gate buys 3 points of precision for 1 of recall.

Both numbers are floors, for the reason in section 3: the ground truth is an SCP code-set
difference, and code sets differ 72% of the time at zero elapsed time.

## 7. Worked examples, graded by a cardiologist

110 PTB-XL reports contain the reading cardiologist's own comparison sentence. For 12, the
prior tracing is also in the dataset and recoverable — so those 12 outputs can be graded
against a physician's account of the same two ECGs rather than my own. Full text and
per-case adjudication in [`examples.md`](examples.md).

| Verdict | n |
|---|---|
| Concordant | 3 |
| Concordant but incomplete | 3 |
| Mixed | 2 |
| Discordant | 4 |

Six of twelve agreed with the cardiologist on the principal change. The best case needed no
prompting to produce the cardiologist's own sentence:

> **Cardiologist:** "atrial fibrillation has reverted to sinus rhythm. st segments are
> depressed in i, ii, v4,5,6."
>
> **APEX:** "Atrial fibrillation has reverted to sinus rhythm. New since the prior study:
> first-degree AV block." — with a measured PR of 242 ms backing the AV block, and a
> refusal to report a PR *change* because the prior study was in AF.

The failures cluster. Three of the four discordant cases are **false positives on stable
studies** — 11737→11740 is the cleanest, where the cardiologist wrote "it is not
significantly changed" and APEX reported three changes, all spurious. The fourth is the
opposite: a real anterior ST elevation that fell under the family-wise bar. And the
successes cluster too: every strong case involved a change *large relative to the noise
floor* — a rhythm conversion, a 0.12 mV ST shift, a disappearing ectopic. **The module is
reliable exactly where the signal is unambiguous**, which is worth knowing before trusting
it where it is not.

## 8. Limitations

- **Absolute intervals are biased** by 100 Hz sampling and the 40 Hz low-pass. Differences
  are the supported output; absolute values are validated (§2) but should not be quoted as
  measurements from a diagnostic ECG machine.
- **The PR plausibility floor (80 ms) is too permissive.** Gold pair 3176→3181 reported PR
  98 → 176 ms, where 98 ms is almost certainly a mis-placed P onset. Tightening it risks
  rejecting genuine pre-excitation, so it is documented rather than changed.
- **T-wave morphology change is not measured.** Cardiologists write "t waves are now low
  rather than inverted" constantly; this module has no channel for it and misses all of it.
- **The AV-dissociation guard depends on the detector** surfacing 2AVB/3AVB, which it did
  not do in the one case that needed it.
- **Change ground truth is annotation-noisy** (§3), so §6's F1 figures are lower bounds.
- **The gold set is 12 pairs**, selected by whether a comparison happened to be dictated
  and the prior happened to be retained. Worked examples with a good reference standard —
  not a benchmark.
- **Two studies only.** Trend across three or more recordings is not modelled.
